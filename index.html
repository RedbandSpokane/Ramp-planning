1

Mark the footprint on the slab

Snap chalk lines for every outline in the drawing. The whole ramp covers 17′ 10″ by 17′ 10″. Check that nothing is in the way, including a door swing or a parked vehicle. Measure both platform diagonals. When they are equal, at 7′ 5⅛″, the platform outline is square.

2

Build the platform frame

Cut the posts to 5¾″. Stand them on post bases and anchor each base to the slab with concrete screws. Build the frame: a header along each end and the joists hung between them 16″ apart on centre. Set it on top of the posts so its top sits 11¼″ above the slab, check it is level and square, and fasten each post to the header with two framing angles.

Header · 5′ 3″ ×2Joist · 5′ ×5Post · 5¾″ ×6
3

Cut and build the stringers for Ramp 1

Cut 4 stringers 12′ 1″ long from 2x6, one board each. At each end saw 4.8° off square so the ends stand plumb. At the low end, rip the bottom edge in a long taper so the stringer lies flat on the slab for the first 6′ 3¼″. Cut the legs to the lengths under the drawing and bevel each top to 4.8°. Stand one under each stringer at each leg row and fasten it with two 3″ screws driven at an angle up through the leg into the stringer.

Cut and build the stringers for Ramp 2

Cut 4 stringers 12′ 1″ long from 2x6, one board each. At each end saw 4.8° off square so the ends stand plumb. Cut the legs to the lengths under the drawing and bevel each top to 4.8°. Stand one under each stringer at each leg row and fasten it with two 3″ screws driven at an angle up through the leg into the stringer.

Stringer · 12′ 1″ ×4Leg 1 · 9¾″ ×4Leg 2 · 13¾″ ×4Leg 3 · 17⅝″ ×4
5

Stand the ramps and add blocking

Stand the stringers up 14½″ apart on centre. Where a ramp meets the platform, fasten each stringer end to the platform rim, the outside board of the platform frame, with two heavy framing angles. Lay the toe blocks flat on the slab between the stringers, 33″ from the low end of Ramp 1. Screw each one to the stringers beside it and anchor it to the slab with two concrete screws, so the low end cannot creep. Screw the end header across the top of Ramp 2 and fasten it to the structure the ramp meets. Add the blocking between the stringers where the framing plan shows it. At the legs the blocks zigzag: neighbouring blocks sit 3″ apart along the ramp, so you can drive screws straight through the stringer into both ends of every block. A scrap of 2x makes a quick spacer between them. Under a plywood joint the blocks must run in a straight line, so both panel edges land on them. There, drive screws straight through the stringer where you can reach its far side, and at an angle where you can’t. Across each row of legs 12″ or taller, screw a diagonal 2x4 brace flat against the downhill face, screwed to every leg it crosses, so the ramp cannot sway sideways. It needs no lengthwise braces, because both ends of every ramp are fastened down.

Install the handrails

Cut the rail posts to the lengths listed. Stand each post against the outside face of the outer stringer, plumb, and fasten it with two carriage bolts. Keep the posts no more than 48 inches apart. Near the low end the stringer is cut away, so those posts stand on the slab in a post base held with concrete screws. Bolt them to the stringer as well wherever it is deep enough. Do the same around the open edges of the platform, bolting the posts to the outside of the platform frame. Fasten the round rail to the top of the posts so its top is 36 inches above the deck. At the top and the bottom of each ramp, add the 12″ level end pieces, joined to the sloped rail with a small miter or a rail fitting. Sand the rail smooth.

    Checks and notes

    Tip. Stringers on Ramp 1 and Ramp 2 come out 12′ 1″ long, just 1″ over a 12′ board. Shorten the run by about 1″ and you can buy the shorter board.

    Side-view drawings stretch the height so the slope is visible. Plan views are to scale.

    How the numbers are worked out
    • Lumber uses real sizes: a 2x6 is 1½ by 5½ inches. Plywood is ¾ inch thick, in 4 by 8 foot sheets.
    • The ramp surface sits on stringers set on edge, 16 inches apart on centre, with the top of each stringer ¾ inch under the surface.
    • Stringers rest on legs, with no more than 48 inches between supports (36 inches for an electric or bariatric chair). Near the slab the stringers are ripped to lie flat on it, and the first leg row starts where they lift off. Leg lengths are measured at the centre of the leg. Bevel each top to the slope angle.
    • Stringers up to 16 feet are one board. Longer ones are made in pieces that meet over a leg, with a 24 inch splice plate across each joint. Odd and even stringers join over different legs, so the joints are staggered and the ramp has no weak line across it.
    • Outdoors, each row of supports is two posts in concrete or on footings with a beam of two 2x6 plies (three 2x8 plies for an electric or bariatric chair) sitting on top in post caps. Where the ramp is too low for posts, short 4x4 legs stand on concrete pavers, and the low end lies on a gravel and paver pad.
    • Support under the plywood: stringers run the full length about 14 to 16 inches apart, with legs no more than 48 inches apart under them. Blocking sits at every leg row and at every plywood joint across the ramp. On decks wider than 48 inches the long seam is moved onto a stringer. On the platform the seam is moved onto a joist, with blocking where a seam crosses the joists. For a heavy power chair, ask a builder about closer stringers or thicker plywood.
    • Chair type: manual uses stringers 16 inches apart and legs every 48 inches. Electric or bariatric tightens that to 12 inches and 36 inches, raises the stringers to 2x8, uses 4x4 legs, and asks for a platform at least 6 feet square and a clear width of 42 inches or more.
    • Handrails: wood is a round rail on 4x4 posts; metal is 1¼ inch galvanized pipe on pipe posts with slip-on fittings. Either way the rail is 36 inches above the deck on posts bolted to the outside of the stringers about every 4 feet. Each rail runs level for 12 inches past the top and bottom of a ramp. Platform edges get rails only when you pick both sides.
    • The curbs are 2x4s on edge, screwed on top of the deck at both edges. Deck width is the clear width plus 3 inches.
    • Lumber counts come from packing every cut into 8 to 16 foot boards, then shrinking each board to the shortest stock length that still holds its cuts. Buy one or two extra boards for mistakes.
    • Code mode: the Code check measures the ramp against the common ADA figures (1:12 slope, 30 inches of rise per run, 60 inch landings, 36 inch width, rails 34 to 38 inches high, 2 inch curbs). Turning the mode on holds the slope at 1:12 or gentler and puts rails on both sides.     • This is a planning tool. It is not engineered, and it does not check a chair and rider against your lumber. For a heavy power chair or a tall platform, have someone qualified look at the design.
