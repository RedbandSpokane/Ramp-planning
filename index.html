Build notes

Tip. Stringers on Ramp 1 and Ramp 2 come out 12′ 1″ long, just 1″ over a 12′ board. Shorten the run by about 1″ and you can buy the shorter board.

Build order

  1. Chalk the footprint on the slab, 17′ 3″ × 17′ 3″, and check the platform corners are square.
  2. Build the platform frame first. Cut the posts to 5¾″, stand them on post bases, and level the headers. Then lay the joists and deck it.
  3. Cut the stringers. Mark the top edge, then saw 4.8° off square for each plumb end. At the slab end, trim the bottom so the stringer sits flat and the tip is no thinner than 1½ inches.
  4. Cut the legs to the lengths listed, bevel each top to 4.8°, and screw them to the stringers at the marked spots.
  5. Stand the stringers up, 14½″ apart on centre. Fasten the high end to the platform or end header, then add the blocking at each leg row.
  6. Glue and screw the plywood down, with every joint landing on a stringer or block. The last few inches at the slab end hang over the stringer tip, so bevel that edge.
  7. Screw the 2x4 curbs on edge along each side, then add the non-slip surface.

Drawings use a vertical scale of ×3.5 in the side view so the slope is visible. The plan view is to scale.

How the numbers are worked out
  • Lumber uses real sizes: a 2x6 is 1½ by 5½ inches. Plywood is ¾ inch thick, in 4 by 8 foot sheets.
  • The ramp surface sits on stringers set on edge, 16 inches apart on centre, with the top of each stringer ¾ inch under the surface.
  • Stringers rest on legs, with no more than 48 inches between supports. Leg lengths are measured at the centre of the leg. Bevel each top to the slope angle.
  • The curbs are 2x4s on edge, screwed on top of the deck at both edges. Deck width is the clear width plus 3 inches.
  • Lumber counts come from packing every cut into 8 to 16 foot boards, then shrinking each board to the shortest stock length that still holds its cuts. Buy one or two extra boards for mistakes.
  • This is a planning tool. It is not engineered, and it does not check a chair and rider against your lumber. For a heavy power chair or a tall platform, have someone qualified look at the design.
