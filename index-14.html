Assembly guide

1

Mark the footprint on the slab

Snap chalk lines for every outline in the drawing. The whole ramp covers 17′ 3″ by 17′ 3″. Check that nothing is in the way, including a door swing or a parked vehicle. Measure both platform diagonals. When they are equal, at 7′ 5⅛″, the platform outline is square.

2

Build the platform frame

Cut the posts to 5¾″. Stand them on post bases and anchor each base to the slab with concrete screws. Screw the headers to the posts so the top of the frame sits 11¼″ above the slab. Hang the joists between the headers 16″ apart on centre, then check the frame is level and square.

P-1 Header × 2 · 5′ 3″P-2 Joist × 5 · 5′P-3 Post × 6 · 5¾″
3

Cut and build the stringers for Ramp 1

Cut 4 stringers 12′ 1″ long from 2x6. At each end saw 4.8° off square so the ends stand plumb. At the slab end, trim the bottom edge so the stringer sits flat on the slab. Cut the legs to the lengths under the drawing, bevel each top to 4.8°, and screw each leg to every stringer at the distance from the low end.

Cut and build the stringers for Ramp 2

Cut 4 stringers 12′ 1″ long from 2x6. At each end saw 4.8° off square so the ends stand plumb. Cut the legs to the lengths under the drawing, bevel each top to 4.8°, and screw each leg to every stringer at the distance from the low end.

5

Stand the ramps and add blocking

Stand the stringers up 14½″ apart on centre. Fasten each stringer end that meets the platform to the platform header with heavy framing angles. Anchor the toe cleat to the slab at the low end of Ramp 1 with concrete screws. Screw the end header across the top of Ramp 2 and fasten it to the structure the ramp meets. Add the blocking between the stringers at every leg row and at every plywood joint.

Checks and notes

Tip. Stringers on Ramp 1 and Ramp 2 come out 12′ 1″ long, just 1″ over a 12′ board. Shorten the run by about 1″ and you can buy the shorter board.

Side-view drawings stretch the height so the slope is visible. Plan views are to scale.

How the numbers are worked out
  • Lumber uses real sizes: a 2x6 is 1½ by 5½ inches. Plywood is ¾ inch thick, in 4 by 8 foot sheets.
  • The ramp surface sits on stringers set on edge, 16 inches apart on centre, with the top of each stringer ¾ inch under the surface.
  • Stringers rest on legs, with no more than 48 inches between supports. Leg lengths are measured at the centre of the leg. Bevel each top to the slope angle.
  • The curbs are 2x4s on edge, screwed on top of the deck at both edges. Deck width is the clear width plus 3 inches.
  • Lumber counts come from packing every cut into 8 to 16 foot boards, then shrinking each board to the shortest stock length that still holds its cuts. Buy one or two extra boards for mistakes.
  • This is a planning tool. It is not engineered, and it does not check a chair and rider against your lumber. For a heavy power chair or a tall platform, have someone qualified look at the design.
